Inside a solar module

Each module is a sandwich of protective layers around the cells that make the power.

Toughened glassProtects the cells from hail, dust and rain while letting sunlight through.
EVA encapsulantSeals and cushions the cells against moisture and vibration.
Solar cellsSilicon cells turn sunlight into DC electricity. Each cell makes roughly half a volt, so they are wired in series.
EVA encapsulantSecond sealing layer behind the cells.
BacksheetElectrical insulation and weather barrier on the rear.
Aluminium frame and junction boxThe frame gives strength and mounting points. The junction box holds bypass diodes and the output cables.

From cell to system

Solar cellAbout 0.5 V each
ModuleCells wired in series
String and arrayModules in series add voltage; strings in parallel add current
InverterConverts DC to AC for use or export
Bypass diodes limit loss when part of a module is shadedStandard connectors make strings quick to wireOutput is rated at standard test conditions (Wp)

Main components

CellsMonocrystalline silicon, PERC or TOPCon type, for high efficiency.
Front glassToughened, low-iron glass for high light transmission.
EncapsulantEVA layers that bond and seal the cell layer.
BacksheetInsulating polymer layer on the rear.
FrameAnodised aluminium with mounting holes.
Junction boxBypass diodes, output cables and connectors, rated for outdoor use.
